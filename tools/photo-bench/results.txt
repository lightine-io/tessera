Full-page photo benchmark, Android saved-image reading (TES-131)

Run 2026-11-21 on the Pixel_9_API37_Play emulator, ML Kit text-recognition 16.0.1 (bundled Latin
model). SDK path: SavedImageMrzReader over mlKitSavedImageRecognizer, which includes the existing
short-row rescue (MlKitRowRescue).
Set: 8 synthetic TD3 passport pages and 8 TD1 ID cards (generator seed 131), 3024 x 4032 JPEG, OCR-B
MRZ at the real position and character pitch, grey portrait placeholder, invented labels, wood desk.
Grade: read = decoded and the raw MRZ lines equal the generated ones; partly = decoded with at least
one wrong character; none = no MRZ found. Variants are defined in render.swift; mrzcrop is the
control (a tight crop around the MRZ). Eight images per cell: read it as a pattern, not a rate.

Reading the tables
- On a flat full page the SDK reads about as well as on the tight crop (11 of 16 against 10 of 16),
  so the page itself does not break reading. Both are well below 16 of 16: most misses are ML Kit's
  filler collapse (long filler runs come back split, shortened, or turned into guillemets).
- Every other variant loses most reads. In most "none" cases ML Kit did return the right number of
  MRZ-like rows, but with the wrong length, so the reader reports no MRZ. This is the failure the
  iOS photo reader had (TES-130).
- The existing short-row rescue only helps when one row reads at exact width. The naive per-row
  crop and re-read helps only the flat page and the crop, and nothing elsewhere. Copying the iOS
  rescue as is would not be enough; the open question is the rows' tilt and the neighbouring
  fragments, not the crop itself.
- Synthetic limits: no real print, lens, or paper. A real device and real photos are the owner's check.
